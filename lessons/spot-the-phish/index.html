    Introduction
    Why it matters

    Attackers don't break in. They get invited.

    Phishing is a message designed to trick you into clicking a link, opening a file, or sharing information. It works because it looks like it came from someone you trust: your IT team, your bank, your boss.

    Firewalls and antivirus catch a lot, but they can't catch everything. The last line of defense is the person reading the email. That's you, and the good news is that a few habits make you very hard to fool.

    The core habit: when a message asks you to act quickly, slow down. Urgency is the attacker's favorite tool, because people who rush don't check.
