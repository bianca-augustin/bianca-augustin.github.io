Not every problem is a training problem

When someone asks for "a training," my first job is to find out what's really getting in the way. Here's how I'd approach four problems almost every workplace has, with a small working demo for each.

Is it a training problem?

A quick version of the questions I ask stakeholders before designing anything. Think of a performance problem at your workplace and answer honestly.

Problem 1

New hires feel lost in their first week

Training + support
What the manager says"We give them a 60-page handbook and a login. By Friday they're overwhelmed, and three of our last ten hires quit within 90 days."

What's really going on

  • Information overload: everything arrives on day one, with no priority.
  • No clear definition of a "successful" first week.
  • New hires don't know who to ask, so they don't ask.
  • Managers are busy and onboarding varies by team.

How I'd measure it

90-day retentionTime to first independent taskWeek-one confidence survey

Creative solutions

The first-week quest

Replace the handbook dump with a short checklist of real tasks, each worth points. New hires see progress, and managers see exactly where someone is stuck.

INTERACTIVE CHECKLIST · TRY IT BELOW

Buddy program with conversation cards

Pair each hire with a peer and give them five prompt cards ("Who do people go to when…?") so the coffee chat is actually useful.

PRINTABLE CARDS · PEER LEARNING

Five-minute daily videos

One short video per day: the culture, the tools, the people. Spaced over the week instead of crammed into orientation.

Problem 2

Everyone clicks through compliance training

Training, redesigned
What HR says"Completion is 98%, so technically we're fine. But people still make the same data privacy mistakes, and nobody remembers the training a month later."

What's really going on

  • The course tests recall of rules, not decisions in real situations.
  • Once a year means forgotten by February.
  • The content feels like it was written for lawyers, not employees.

How I'd measure it

Policy incidents per quarterChallenge participationScenario accuracy over time

Creative solutions

Decisions instead of slides

Rebuild the course around realistic situations where the "right" answer isn't obvious, like my Spot the Phish course.

BRANCHING SCENARIOS

The two-minute weekly challenge

One quick scenario per week, delivered in email or chat. Spaced practice beats one long annual session.

SPACED MICROLEARNING · TRY IT BELOW

Manager huddle cards

A five-minute discussion prompt managers use in team meetings, so the conversation keeps going after the course.

Problem 3

Managers avoid tough feedback conversations

Practice needed
What the VP says"Our managers know they should give feedback. They took the workshop. But problems sit for months, and then someone gets a surprise at review time."

What's really going on

  • Knowing a model isn't the same as saying the words out loud.
  • Fear of the other person's reaction, with no safe place to practice.
  • Vague feedback ("be more proactive") that nobody can act on.

How I'd measure it

Feedback frequency (pulse survey)Review-time surprisesManager confidence rating

Creative solutions

Conversation simulator

Managers practice a realistic conversation, choose what to say, and see how the employee reacts. Mistakes are free here.

BRANCHING DIALOGUE · TRY IT BELOW

The SBI pocket card

Situation, Behavior, Impact. A one-page job aid with sentence starters managers can glance at before a conversation.

JOB AID

Peer practice labs

Monthly 30-minute sessions where managers role-play real (anonymized) situations in pairs.

Problem 4

Expense reports keep getting rejected

Not a training problem
What Finance says"Forty percent of expense reports come back for corrections. We need a mandatory training on the expense policy."

What's really going on

  • People submit reports a few times a year, so nobody remembers the rules.
  • The most common errors are a missing receipt and the wrong category.
  • The form allows mistakes it could easily prevent.
  • A course would be forgotten long before the next report.

How I'd measure it

Rejection rateFinance hours spent on correctionsTime to reimbursement

Creative solutions

Fix the form first

Make the receipt upload required and add plain-language category descriptions. Prevent the error instead of training people to avoid it.

PROCESS FIX · TRY IT BELOW

Inline help, right where it's needed

Short tips next to each field ("Client dinner? Use Meals: Client and list attendees"). Help shows up at the moment of need.

PERFORMANCE SUPPORT

A 60-second pre-submit checklist

Three questions before "Submit": receipt attached? Category matches? Attendees listed for meals?
