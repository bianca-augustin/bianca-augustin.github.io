How I work

From problem to performance

A simple process I adapt to the project, whether it's a two-week job aid or a full course.

Listen

Ask a lot of questions. Talk to the people doing the work and find the real gap: skill, knowledge, motivation, or environment.

Map

Define the observable behaviors that matter, then design practice for each one. Cut the content that doesn't serve them.

Prototype

Build a quick working version early, test it with real learners, and revise before polishing.

Measure

Plan evaluation up front: completion and scores, yes, but mostly whether behavior on the job changed.

About me

I started in the classroom, and I still design like a teacher.

My career began as a Career and Technical Education (CTE) teacher with the New York City Department of Education. I earned my work-based learning coordinator license and went on to run work-based learning programs, placing students in jobs, internships, and job shadows.

Much of that work was in District 79, New York City's alternative schools and programs. That's where I learned that accessibility isn't something you add at the end. It's how you build an equitable learning environment for every learner from the very start.

Today I design learning for higher education, college, and adult learners. I love making teaching and learning fun, because engaged learners remember more. And I love finding creative, effective solutions, especially to problems that don't look like training problems at first.

My real strength is asking the right questions. I'm a strong project manager and team leader, and I work hard to create a safe environment where learners and teammates feel comfortable trying, failing, and trying again.
